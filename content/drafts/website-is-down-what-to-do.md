**The short answer:** first confirm it's down for everyone, not only you. Then check the three most common causes: an expired domain, unpaid hosting, or a recent change that broke something. Most outages come from one of these and can be fixed the same day.

## Work through these in order

1. **Check it isn't just you.** Try your phone on mobile data, then a checker such as [downforeveryoneorjustme.com](https://downforeveryoneorjustme.com). If it loads elsewhere, the problem is your network or browser.
2. **Check your domain.** Log in where you bought it and look at the expiry date. Renewal reminders often go to an old email address or to whoever built the site. Most registrars give you a short grace period to renew, so act immediately.
3. **Check your hosting.** Look for failed payments or a suspended account, then check your host's status page for an outage.
4. **Think about what changed.** A plugin update, a new theme or a DNS edit just before the site went down is your likely culprit. Undo it or restore a backup.
5. **Read the error on the screen.** It's a useful clue.

## What the error usually means

| What you see | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| **"This site can't be reached"** or a DNS error | Domain expired, or DNS settings changed |
| **A parking page or an ad page** | Domain expired |
| **"Account suspended"** | Hosting unpaid, or a policy problem |
| **500 Internal Server Error** | A broken plugin, update or code change |
| **502, 503 or 504 errors** | The server is overloaded or down |
| **White screen** | A crash, often on WordPress after an update |
| **A red warning from your browser** | Possible malware or an expired security certificate |

If the site redirects to strange pages or shows content you didn't add, treat it as hacked: change your passwords and get help straight away.

## While it's down

Pin your phone number or WhatsApp on your Google Business Profile and social pages so clients can still reach you. Don't keep making changes at random, because that can make recovery harder. Write down what you've tried.

## Afterwards

Put your domain on auto-renew with an email address you check, keep a working payment method on file, make sure regular backups exist, and set up a free uptime monitor so you hear about the next outage first.

## Questions

### Why did my website suddenly stop working?

The most common reasons are an expired domain, an unpaid hosting bill, a hosting outage, or an update that broke something. Check them in that order. Sites rarely fail for no reason, and something has usually changed or lapsed.

### Will being down hurt my Google ranking?

A few hours rarely matters. If a site stays down for days, Google can start dropping pages from its results, though they usually return once the site is back. Fix it quickly and check Search Console afterwards.
